Trust, parts & warranty

Your phone isn't just a phone.

It's your photos, your messages, your work, your banking. You're handing all of that to a stranger in a parking lot. Here's exactly what I do with it, what goes inside your device, and what happens if something goes wrong.

The wizard's privacy oath

Four commitments about your data. They don't change based on who's asking.

I don't go through your device
Not your photos, messages, notes or apps. A screen or battery job needs none of that.
I don't copy anything off it
Nothing is backed up, transferred or stored by me. Your device leaves with the data it arrived with.
I don't keep your passcode
If a repair needs one I ask at the time and don't write it down. Change it afterwards if you'd rather.
Back up before I arrive
Repairs shouldn't touch your data, but hardware work carries risk. I can't take responsibility for data loss.

When would you actually need my passcode?

Only to test that the repair worked. After a screen replacement I need to confirm touch responds across the whole panel, the cameras focus, Face ID or the fingerprint reader still authenticates, and the speakers and mics work. Most of that needs the phone unlocked.

You have two alternatives, and I'm happy with either: stay with me and unlock it yourself when I ask, or take it back locked and test it yourself before you pay. If you skip testing, you're accepting the repair as-is.

What actually goes in your phone

Most shops won't tell you this before you pay. You should know it before you decide.

I fit professional-grade aftermarket parts, not original Apple or Samsung components.

They come from MobileSentrix and Injured Gadgets — two of the main distributors independent repair shops in the US buy from. They're good parts. They are not the part your phone shipped with, and on modern phones that has consequences you deserve to hear up front.

Your iPhone will log the repair

After an aftermarket screen or battery, iOS records a non-genuine part under Settings → General → About. It's a notice, not a fault, and it doesn't stop the phone working or updating. It doesn't go away.

True Tone usually stops working

True Tone data is tied to the original display. Restoring it needs a programmer and board-level IC transfer, which I don't do. If that feature matters to you, an Apple Store repair is the honest answer and I'll tell you so.

Battery health may stop showing

iOS often hides the battery health percentage after an aftermarket battery. The battery charges and holds normally — you just lose the readout.

Face ID and fingerprint keep working

Those survive a screen job as long as the original sensor assembly transfers across cleanly. That's careful handling, not a special tool. If I find it damaged, you hear about it before I go further.

Why tell you all this? Because you'll find it in your settings a week later anyway, and I'd rather you heard it from me than concluded I'd hidden it. If any of it is a dealbreaker, that's a completely reasonable call — say so and I'll point you where you need to go.

The 30-day ward, in plain terms

Every repair is covered for 30 days from the day I finish it, on both the part and my work.

Covered

A replacement part that fails or turns out defective. Workmanship — anything I fitted badly or didn't seat properly. A fault that traces back to the repair itself: dead pixels, touch dropping out, a port that works loose, a battery that won't hold.

You get it fixed at no charge. If the same part fails twice, I refund the labor.

Not covered

A new drop. A fresh crack in the replacement screen. Liquid contact after the repair. Any other accidental damage. Faults in a part of the device I never worked on.

Physical damage to a replacement screen is the common one, and I can usually tell from the impact pattern. I'll show you what I'm looking at rather than just saying no.

How to claim

Text me or use the orb, tell me what's happening, and we set a time — same as booking anything else. No form, no receipt hunting. I know what I fitted and when.

Common questions

Data, parts and warranty are answered in full above. Everything else is here.

Are you a real wizard, or just a weird tech guy?+

A healthy mix of both. The wizard persona makes a stressful situation fun. The repair standards behind it are dead serious — your device is a piece of machinery worth restoring, not a disposable.

How long does a repair take?+

About an hour and a half for most jobs — screens, batteries, charging ports, cameras — and most finish sooner. The exception is back glass on iPhone X through 14, which takes at least two days because it needs laser separation I don't have the equipment for yet. Everything else, including back glass on Android and iPhone 15 and up, is same-day. If a part has to be ordered first, add 3–5 days for shipping.

Where do repairs happen?+

I come to you anywhere around Wichita — home, office, or a public spot if you'd rather. For first-time customers I'll usually suggest somewhere public, like a coffee shop or a library lot.

What if I need to cancel, or want a refund?+

If your part hasn't been ordered yet, you get a full refund, no questions. If it has, a $10 cancellation fee covers the non-returnable order and the rest of your deposit comes back. You're never charged for labor until the repair is actually done.

Can I just call you?+

Calls to 316-559-4816 go to voicemail — I don't take live calls, so I can keep my hands on the bench. Text, email, or the orb all reach me directly and get a faster reply.

What devices do you work on?+

iPhone, Samsung Galaxy and Google Pixel are the bulk of it — screens, batteries, charging ports, cameras and back glass. Other Android phones and tablets depend on whether parts are available for that model, so send me the model and I'll check. I don't do laptops or consoles.

Do you fix water damage?+

Only if the device still shows some sign of life — a flicker, a vibration, anything. If it's completely dead that's board-level work needing a microsoldering bench I don't have, and I'll say so instead of charging you to find out.

Do you work on Face ID and fingerprint sensors?+

They keep working through a screen repair as long as the original sensor assembly transfers across cleanly, which is normal careful handling. What I can't do is repair a Face ID module that's already failed — that's board-level.

What if you can't fix it, or you damage my phone?+

If I can't fix it, you owe nothing for labor. If I damage something during the repair, I make it right — that's what the workmanship half of the 30-day ward is for. I'd rather tell you a job is beyond me before starting than hand you back something worse.

Do I need an appointment?+

Yes, but it's just a text. There's no shop to walk into — I come to you, so we agree a time and place first.

How do I pay, and when?+

You pay when the repair is finished and tested, never before. The one exception is special-order parts, which need a deposit covering the part cost so I'm not out of pocket if the job falls through. I'll confirm which payment methods I take when we set the time.

What areas do you cover?+

Wichita and the nearby towns — Derby, Andover, Haysville, Maize, Goddard, Park City, Bel Aire, Rose Hill and Mulvane. Further out, just ask and I'll tell you honestly whether the drive works.
